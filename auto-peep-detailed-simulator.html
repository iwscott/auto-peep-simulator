What the pressure animation means

In this idealised passive, single-compartment model, airway opening pressure during constant-flow inspiration is approximately Paw = set PEEP + volume / compliance + resistance × flow. In VC with constant inspiratory flow, the last term is the resistive pressure drop. In PC, a fixed inspiratory pressure drives decelerating flow and delivered volume depends on resistance, compliance and inspiratory time. It is not alveolar distension pressure.

When you press Inspiratory hold, inspiratory flow stops, the resistance × flow component disappears and proximal airway pressure approaches alveolar pressure (plateau pressure). Compare the pressure labels and the yellow waveform before and during the hold.

With increased resistance and too little expiratory time, lung volume may remain above its baseline at the next breath. The alveolar cluster stays enlarged to illustrate gas trapping. Press Expiratory hold at the end of expiration to display estimated total PEEP; intrinsic PEEP is total PEEP minus set PEEP.

Use the Controls tab to adjust rate, inspiratory time, resistance, compliance, tidal volume, set PEEP and playback speed. Use Next breath to advance one complete breath while paused. The airway labels show a simplified illustrative gradient, not measured pressures in real anatomical locations.

Limitations: Real bronchospasm is heterogeneous and can involve dynamic airway closure, flow limitation, nonuniform time constants, spontaneous effort, pendelluft and incomplete equilibration during holds. This model does not calculate regional lung stress or provide patient-specific clinical predictions. In pressure control, peak and plateau can be equal if inspiratory flow reaches zero; a pressure drop is not guaranteed.
